Why This Source Matters

A statement of intent

Because the interview appeared as FIFA 14 was being released, it records how EA publicly explained its soundtrack strategy at the end of this exhibit’s period. Schnur presents discovery as a deliberate goal rather than an accidental result of choosing enjoyable music.

As you read, notice how he describes listeners as groups that can be connected: established fans with unfamiliar artists, one region with another, and football culture with music scenes that local radio might not reach.

Featured Statement

“Our primary objective is to listen globally.”
— Steve Schnur

From Soundtrack to Showcase

Discovery was part of the design

Schnur describes EA’s task as more than finding songs that matched the energy of football. The music team wanted a soundtrack that could feel current through months of repeated play while also introducing performers who were not yet familiar to every part of FIFA’s audience.

FIFA 14 mixed recognizable acts with rising artists such as CHVRCHES, Disclosure, Los Rakas, and Chinza Dopeness. Familiar names could give listeners an entry point, while less familiar musicians received access to the same worldwide game audience.

The Cross-Border Model

Local music scene→EA selection
FIFA menu rotation→Repeated exposure
One national market→International players

Crossing Musical Borders

Broader access without an active search

EA did not require players to seek out every featured artist. Songs appeared during lineup changes, menu navigation, and other repeated routines. An unfamiliar track could therefore become recognizable before a player knew the performer’s name.

This matters because placement could move music beyond the radio formats, national markets, and genre communities in which it began. FIFA’s audience turned a menu soundtrack into a distribution route linking local performers with listeners elsewhere.
